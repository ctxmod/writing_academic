# The Author-Date In-Text Shape

In the APA 7 family, the in-text citation's information core is the
author and the year. The two travel together wherever the citation
appears. The parenthetical placement credits the finding: `(Strand,
2019)` sits in its own parentheses at the end of the clause. The
narrative placement credits the researcher: the year alone moves into
parentheses when the author's name is part of the sentence, as in
`Strand (2019) followed the cohort for a decade`. Varying between the
two placements keeps a document's citations from settling into one
mechanical pattern.

## Placement at the format-family level

Both placements carry the same two data, author and year, and both
resolve against the same alphabetical reference list. A page or
section locator accompanies direct quotations in this family, and the
locator rides inside the parentheses after the year, as in `(Strand,
2019, p. 42)`. The locator never replaces the author-date pair.

One style decision belongs to the document level: the author-date
pair is written the same way at every citation site, parenthetical or
narrative, first appearance or repeat. A work with three or more
authors is cited by the first author and "et al." from the first
citation on, `(Strand et al., 2019)`, unless that shortening makes two
works indistinguishable.
