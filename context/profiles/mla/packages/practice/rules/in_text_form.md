# The Author-Page In-Text Shape

In the MLA 9 family, the in-text citation identifies its source by the
author and the page, and the pair stands without intervening
punctuation in the parenthetical form: `(Strand 42)`. When the
author's name is part of the sentence, the parentheses carry the page
alone: `Strand reads the archive as a pattern of delays (42)`. Author
and page are the shape's whole information core; the work resolves
through the works-cited list, where the reader searches by author.

## Placement at the format-family level

The parenthetical form credits the finding and sits before the
sentence's closing punctuation. The narrative form credits the
researcher and suits the sentence whose subject is the work's
argument. Both forms resolve against the same alphabetical
works-cited list.

The family carries no year inside the citation, and no comma between
author and page: author and page suffice, which is the family's
deliberate contrast with the author-date styles. Page locators
belong to quotations and to references to specific passages.
