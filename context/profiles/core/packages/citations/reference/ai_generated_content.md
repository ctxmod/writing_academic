# Citing AI-Generated Content

All three general-purpose manuals now publish guidance for citing
output from a generative AI tool. They agree on the posture: the
writer says how the tool was used, cites any output that is quoted,
paraphrased, or built into the work, and never presents the tool as
a human author. They differ in the shape of the entry, and the
document's one style decides which shape applies, under the `Hold One
Style` rule.

## The three treatments

| Style | Who stands as author | The entry | A chat others cannot open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| APA 7 | The company that makes the tool | Company, date, tool or model in italics with a bracketed description, URL. A specific chat adds its date, title, and share link | Still a reference-list entry: APA treats the output as an algorithm's output, not personal communication |
| MLA 9 | No author: the tool is never an author | Description of the prompt, the tool as container, the model as version, company, date, share link | The tool's general URL stands in for the missing share link |
| Chicago 18 | The developer, when an entry is given | A note or text citation for the specific output; a chat with a public link may be cited like a web page | Treated like a personal communication: cited in a note or the text, no bibliography entry |

## What every style asks

- Disclose the use: Chicago 18 makes the author responsible for
  stating whether and how far AI was used (4.51, 4.76), and the APA
  and MLA guidance assume the same disclosure in the methods, a note,
  or the text.
- Keep the prompts and the output: APA does not require every prompt
  to be cited, but long exchanges may go to an appendix or
  supplemental materials, and MLA and Chicago both prefer a share link
  a reader can open.
- Vet what the tool cites: a source the tool names is not cited until
  the writer has read it. Citing it unread is citing a secondary
  source, and MLA points to its indirect-source rule for that case.
- Follow the venue first: many journals and publishers set their own
  AI policies, and the venue's author instructions outrank a manual's
  default, as the `Benchmark Before Choosing` rule orders.
