# Hold One Style

A document carries exactly one citation style, chosen before drafting
begins and held from the first citation to the last list entry. The
style names a family of decisions that travel together: how an in-text
citation identifies its source, what the closing list is called, how its
entries are ordered, and where locators sit. Mixing decisions from two
styles breaks all of them at once: an author-page citation pointing into
an alphabetical author-date reference list cannot be resolved by a
reader of either convention.

## How drift starts

Mixed-style drift rarely starts as a decision. It starts as a habit: a
writer moves between venues, reuses a paragraph from an earlier paper,
or lets a reference manager reformat entries under a different default.
One imported paragraph carries its old citation family with it, and the
document now teaches the reader two conventions without announcing
either. The failure is structural, not cosmetic. A reader who searches
the closing list under the wrong key finds nothing where a citation
promised a source.

## How drift is caught

The check is mechanical and belongs at review time, not write time:

- every in-text citation uses the discipline's one identification
  pattern;
- the closing list has one name, one ordering, and one entry shape;
- every citation's identifier appears in the list under that shape.

Each discipline profile in this module carries its in-text and list
shapes; compare a draft's citations against the profile's two shapes
before submission. When a style family carries more than one system,
the system is chosen once per document, verified before drafting, and
held through the last citation.
