# Chicago 18: The Author-Date Variant

Chicago 18's author-date system cites like the APA family at the
format-family level, with one difference in punctuation. The in-text
citation is an author-year pair with no comma between author and
year: `(Strand 2019)` in the parenthetical placement, or `Strand
(2019)` in the narrative one. A page or section locator for
quotations follows the year after a comma, inside the parentheses:
`(Strand 2019, 42)`. The closing list is a reference list ordered
alphabetically by family name, one entry per cited work. A work with
more than two authors is cited in the text by the first author and
"et al." (`(Strand et al. 2019, 42)`). The reference list names up to
six authors, and for more than six it gives the first three followed
by "et al."

## When a venue prefers it

The two systems serve different venue conventions, and the venue
settles the choice, never the document's preference. A venue's author
instructions name the system when one is required; doubtful cases fall
to the benchmark, and the Chicago vocabulary helps: venues that say
notes or bibliography mean the notes-bibliography system, while venues
that say reference list or name-date mean author-date. The
verification procedure is this profile's navigation reference's
subject, and the benchmark itself is the core package's `Benchmark
Before Choosing` rule. The comma that APA places between author and
year is the drift signal to check first when a paragraph moves
between an APA draft and a Chicago one. The 18th edition's other
changes are listed in this profile's navigation reference.
